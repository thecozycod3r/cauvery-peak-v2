Brewing

Brew better coffee.

Good coffee is mostly not about the machine. It is about grind, ratio, water temperature and time — and all four are free to get right.

Six rules first

Before the method, the fundamentals.

  • Store coffee in an airtight container, as whole bean, somewhere cool and dark.
  • Grind immediately before you brew. This matters more than almost anything else.
  • The grinder makes or breaks the cup. Spend money here before you spend it on a brewer.
  • Too coarse and the coffee under-extracts — weak and thin. Too fine and it over-extracts — bitter and harsh.
  • The coffee-to-water ratio is what makes a cup strong or watery. Change one variable at a time.
  • Use off-the-boil water, 91–96°C, and the closer to 96°C the better. Never boiling — it scorches the grounds.

Indian Filter

Grind
Medium
Coffee
7–8 g
Water
177 ml
Temp
91–96°C
Time
~10 min
  1. Boil water and let it sit 30 seconds, bringing it down to 91–96°C.
  2. Place the brew basket inside the brewer. Add one level spoon of coffee per cup.
  3. Level the grounds gently, then place the plunger inside the basket.
  4. Pour the water into the basket up to the neck. Close the lid.
  5. In about 10 minutes the decoction is ready. Remove the basket.
  6. Add milk and sugar to taste.

Pour Over

Grind
Medium
Coffee
28 g
Water
350 ml
Temp
91–96°C
Time
~3 min
  1. Add the ground coffee to the dripper and set it on a mug or carafe.
  2. Pour just enough water to moisten the grounds evenly. You will see the bloom as CO₂ releases.
  3. After about 15 seconds, pour in a spiral from the outside in.
  4. Keep pouring slowly into the middle, holding the water level steady.
  5. Let it all drip through, then serve.

Moka Pot

Grind
Medium
Coffee
23 g
Water
330 ml
Temp
Cold to start
Time
~5 min
  1. Take off the top chamber and remove the filter basket.
  2. Fill the bottom chamber with fresh water to just below the valve. Warm, not hot.
  3. Seat the filter basket and fill it with coffee. Do not tamp — level it and brush the rim clean.
  4. Screw the top on. Tighten, but not hard.
  5. Put it on low heat with the lid open so you can watch.
  6. When a brown pressurised stream starts, lower the heat further.
  7. When the stream turns blonde and bubbles, take it off and close the lid.

French Press

Grind
Medium–coarse
Coffee
28 g
Water
350 ml
Temp
91–96°C
Time
4 min
  1. Remove the plunger and add the coffee to the beaker.
  2. Pour about a third of the water over the grounds, saturating all of them.
  3. Let it bloom for 30 seconds — it will expand and bubble.
  4. Pour in the rest of the water.
  5. Rest the plunger on top without pressing. Steep 4 minutes.
  6. Press down slowly and straight, with steady pressure.
  7. Pour it all out. Decant what you are not drinking, or it keeps extracting.

Cold Brew

Grind
Coarse
Coffee
226 g
Water
1.5 L
Temp
Room temp
Time
12–24 hrs
  1. Add the coarse grounds to a large container.
  2. Pour over room-temperature or cold filtered water.
  3. Stir gently so every ground is wet.
  4. Cover and leave at room temperature for 12 to 24 hours.
  5. Strain through a fine sieve lined with cheesecloth into a clean container.
  6. Keep the concentrate refrigerated up to 2 weeks.
  7. Serve diluted 1:1 with water or milk, adjusted to taste. Ice optional.

AeroPress

Grind
Medium–fine
Coffee
15–18 g
Water
200 ml
Temp
91–96°C
Time
~1.5 min
  1. Put a paper filter in the cap and rinse it with hot water.
  2. Assemble in the standard position and set it on your mug.
  3. Add the coffee to the chamber.
  4. Start a timer and pour in the water.
  5. Stir for about 10 seconds.
  6. Seat the plunger to make a seal, but do not press.
  7. At about 1 to 1.5 minutes total, press down slowly over 20–30 seconds.
  8. Stop when you hear the hiss.
