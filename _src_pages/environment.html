The ecosystem

Coffee that grows with the forest.

This is not a sustainability claim. It is a description of how the estate physically works — and the reason the coffee tastes the way it does.

In harmony, literally

Coffee, fruit and spice grown inside the existing forest.

Moderate, evenly distributed rainfall. High elevation. Mixed shade trees in two tiers. Mist that delays the ripening of the Arabica cherry and gives the bean time to develop. The fruit and spice interplanted between the coffee add shade of their own — and lend the cup a flavour profile you cannot get from a cleared field.

Shade

Two tiers of native and exotic shade trees are maintained across the estate. They regulate sunlight and rain, hold the temperature steady, slow weed growth and keep pests down. They are also the reason the cherry ripens slowly enough to develop flavour.

Water

The estate drinks what falls on it. Rainwater is channelled through elaborate stone-lined drains and harvested into water bodies across the property, which then carry the estate through the dry months.

Soil

Vermicompost made on the estate supplies 40% of our fertiliser. Fallen leaf litter feeds the natural sandy loam. The steep slopes are terraced with stone soil-conservation revetments built to stop topsoil washing off the hill.

Biodiversity

The shade trees and water bodies are home to rare birds, mammals, fish and reptiles. They share the ground with the coffee, the fruit and the spices in a micro-climate that exists because the canopy was never cleared.
